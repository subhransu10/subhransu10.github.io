About

I build the representations that let a person see through a robot: LiDAR SLAM, volumetric and object-level semantic maps, streamed live into an immersive interface a human can act on.

I am a PhD candidate at the University of Genova and Leonardo S.p.A., supervised by Gianni Viardo Vercelli and Navvab Kashiri. My PhD develops human-in-the-loop representations that connect real-time LiDAR and RGB-D perception to an XR teleoperation interface. I moved our mapping stack from 2D occupancy grids to online volumetric reconstruction, then added instance-level semantics on top of the SLAM backbone.

I am now working on a hierarchical 3D scene graph, extending it toward dynamic objects and operator-side editing in XR. In parallel I train sparse 4D convolutional networks for moving-object and multi-scan segmentation and deploy them on Husky-class robots.

Every system here is measured against a human study, a benchmark or a live robot, not only a demo video.

Research

Seeing through the robot: XR teleoperation

IEEE/ASME AIM 2026 · first author

An XR cockpit for a Clearpath Husky that fuses two RealSense streams, a Velodyne VLP-16, ROS 2 and Unity, with directional vibrotactile feedback tied to obstacle proximity. Tested with 20 participants against a camera-only baseline.

From flat grids to volumetric, semantic maps

PhD thread · ongoing

A 2D grid discards height, overhang and clearance, exactly what an operator needs. I extended the stack to an online LEO-SLAM voxel map streamed into Unity/VR at interaction rates, with a local 3D instance-level semantic layer on top.

STABLE: stability as a requirement

SeMaNa workshop, IROS 2026 · submitted

An object that flickers in an operator's headset is worse than none. STABLE ties an instance-level semantic layer to a SLAM backbone and shows objects only after their estimated state converges, trading latency for trust.
